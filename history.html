Reorg Driven Development

About This Manifesto

A brief and probably unnecessary history of how RDD came to exist.

It Started with a Meeting

There was no conference. No ski resort. No carefully selected group of organizational transformation experts.

There was a meeting.

A meeting scheduled to explain the latest reorganization.

The invitation promised an overview of the new structure, the rationale behind the changes and what everyone could expect going forward.

Attendance was expected.

The Presentation

The presentation covered the usual topics:

  • Organizational efficiency.
  • Strategic alignment.
  • Simplification of reporting structures.
  • Elimination of duplicated responsibilities.
  • Cost optimization and operational synergies.

The expected savings were clear. The implementation details were less important.

Nobody seemed particularly concerned about the time required for people to understand their new responsibilities, rebuild relationships, transfer knowledge or figure out who was actually doing what.

After all, those were temporary costs.

"The new structure will make us more efficient."

The Economics

The financial logic was particularly elegant.

Removing positions immediately reduced costs. Moving people between teams created synergies. Reassigning responsibilities eliminated duplication.

The productivity dip during the transition was considered an implementation detail.

Ramp-up time was not a cost. It was an investment in the future.

And the savings?

Those were already on the slide.

"The savings are immediate. The benefits will follow."

The Pattern

Someone noticed that this wasn't an isolated event.

Every time delivery became difficult, the organization changed.

Teams were split, merged, renamed or redistributed. Responsibilities moved. Reporting lines changed. New leadership structures appeared.

And each time, the organization expected things to become more efficient.

The previous reorganization had simply not been the right one.

"This time, the structure will be different."

The Next Day

The following morning, someone asked an AI to turn the meeting's underlying philosophy into a software development methodology.

Naturally, nobody objected.

There was another reorganization to prepare for.

The draft was surprisingly close.

They edited it, added a few principles and arrived at something that sounded ridiculous enough to be true.

"Reorganizations over solutions."
"New reporting lines over clear responsibilities."
"Cost reductions over continuity."

And eventually:

"Productivity lost during a reorganization does not count toward its cost if nobody includes it in the business case."

The Website

Then they needed somewhere to put the methodology.

Nobody wanted to build a platform for it.

So they created another static website and put it on GitHub Pages.

Fast, cheap and almost no infrastructure.

"We already have a template for that."

Reusing an existing website was considered an efficiency gain.

Naturally, the new website would have its own URL.

And That Is How RDD Was Born

There was no grand design.

Just a series of organizational decisions made under understandable business constraints.

Each one promised greater efficiency.

Each one introduced a new transition.

Each transition required people to figure out how to work together again.

"The organization chart changed overnight. The organization itself will take a little longer."

And that is Reorg Driven Development.

Postscript

Some details of this history may have been simplified, reconstructed or improved for narrative purposes.

The meeting, however, actually happened.

The reorganization was entirely real.

The projected savings were impressive.

The ramp-up costs remain under evaluation.
